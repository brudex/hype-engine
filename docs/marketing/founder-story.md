# Why I built and open-sourced Hype Engine

Hype Engine did not begin as a grand plan to build another social media tool.
It began with me trying to find one that worked for me.

I started with Postiz. It was capable, but I found it difficult to figure out.
Then I moved to Mixpost. I liked it, but as I used the free version more, I
started running into its limits. After that I tried Blotato. It solved some of
the problems, but the cost was becoming difficult to justify.

At some point, I realized I was moving between products—and paying more—while
still looking for the same thing: a straightforward way to automate social
media posting without giving up control. So I decided to build it.

That became Hype Engine: a free, open-source, self-hosted platform for makers,
teams, and businesses that want to keep their products visible without
manually publishing every post. You can plan and schedule a post once, repeat
it at daily or weekly intervals, or call Hype Engine's API from an automation
tool such as n8n.

Today, Hype Engine supports publishing to X, LinkedIn, Facebook, and Instagram.
The visual, drag-and-drop workflow builder—inspired by tools like n8n—is still
being built, and TikTok support is on the roadmap.

Version 0.1.0 is an early release, and I am building it in public because I
want the useful version of this product to be shaped by the people who actually
need it. That means clearer documentation, more reliable integrations, better
tests, a smoother Docker setup, and workflows that solve real publishing
problems—not a roadmap hidden behind a pricing page.

If you have ever spent too much time learning a posting tool, hit the limits of
a free plan, or watched another subscription eat into your product budget,
Hype Engine is for you. [Try the live demo](https://hypeengine.cachetechs.com),
[run it with Docker](../../README.md#quick-start), or pick up a
[`good first issue`](https://github.com/brudex/hype-engine/issues?q=is%3Aissue%20state%3Aopen%20label%3A%22good%20first%20issue%22).
I would love your feedback—and your help building what comes next.
